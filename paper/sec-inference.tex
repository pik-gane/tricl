\section{Likelihood and estimation}\label{sec:inference}

\subsection{Log-likelihood of an event sequence}
Let $(t_i, \ell_i, \tau_i)_{i=1}^m$ be a fully observed sequence of events in an observation window
$[0, T]$, starting from a known initial network. Between events the network is constant, so the total
rate $\Lambda(t) = \sum_{\text{possible events}} \lambda$ is piecewise constant, and the log-likelihood
of the sequence under parameters $\theta$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logl}
\begin{split}
  L(\theta) ={}& \sum_{i:\ \lambda_i < \infty} \log \lambda_{\tau_i}(\ell_i, t_i^-; \theta)
  - \int_0^T \Lambda(t; \theta)\,dt \\
  &+ \sum_{i:\ \lambda_i = \infty} \log \frac{1}{m_i},
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where the last sum runs over immediate events and $m_i$ is the number of immediate events pending
when event $i$ happened (their order is uniformly random). The integral is a sum of
$\Lambda \times$ waiting time over the inter-event intervals and the final interval up to $T$.
\tricl\ maintains $\Lambda$ incrementally, recomputes it exactly every $2^{16}$ events against
floating-point drift, and accumulates Eq.~\eqref{eq:logl} during simulation. In \emph{replay mode}
(option \ttt{--events-in}) it reads an event sequence from a csv file instead of drawing events,
applies them in order, checks that each is possible under the model, and reports $L(\theta)$. Replay
draws no random numbers.

\subsection{Gradient}
By Eq.~\eqref{eq:rate}, $\lambda$ is linear in the attempt-rate coefficients and depends on the
probability-unit coefficients through $f$. With $n_k$ the angle counts of an event,
\begin{widetext}
\begin{align*}
\frac{\partial L}{\partial a_{\tau k}} &= \sum_{i:\ \tau_i = \tau} \frac{n_k(\ell_i)}{A_i}
   - \int_0^T \sum_{\ell} n_k(\ell, t)\, f(B_\ell(t))\,dt, \\
\frac{\partial L}{\partial b_{\tau k}} &= \sum_{i:\ \tau_i = \tau} \frac{n_k(\ell_i) f'(B_i)}{f(B_i)}
   - \int_0^T \sum_{\ell} n_k(\ell, t)\, A_\ell(t)\, f'(B_\ell(t))\,dt,
\end{align*}
\end{widetext}
with sums over the possible events $\ell$ of type $\tau$ (and $n_k \equiv 1$ for the basic coefficients
$a^0_\tau, b^0_\tau$). The integrands are ``exposures'' that \tricl\ maintains like the total rate, so
the full gradient costs about as much as the likelihood itself (option \ttt{--grad}). Metaparameters
enter the coefficients through expressions; the Python driver \ttt{tricl\_fit.py} maps the gradient to
metaparameters by the chain rule, with the Jacobian from finite differences of the parsed
coefficients, and maximizes $L$ with L-BFGS-B \citep{byrd1995}. Standard errors come from the observed
information, the negative Hessian obtained by finite differences of the analytic gradient.

For fixed probability units, $L$ is concave in the attempt rates (a sum of logarithms of linear
functions minus a linear function). In the probability units it is not concave in general, because $f$
is sigmoidal; Sec.~\ref{sec:application} shows a case where the profile likelihood is not unimodal. Two identifiability
limits are structural: for an event type without any varying angle count only the product
$a^0_\tau f(b^0_\tau)$ is identified; and an angle type's attempt-rate and probability-unit coefficients
are separately identified only if its count varies enough across events.
